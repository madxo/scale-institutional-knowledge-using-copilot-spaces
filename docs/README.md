# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

## Overview

OctoAcme follows an iterative, cross-functional project management model designed to move from idea to delivery with clear ownership and shared accountability. Work begins with project initiation, where teams define the problem, stakeholders, success metrics, and a lightweight project one-pager so there is alignment before committing to planning. Once the initiative is approved, the team turns it into an actionable backlog with prioritized work, estimates, dependencies, and a definition of done. Delivery then happens in short cycles with regular demos, standups, and project tracking to keep progress visible and blockers managed early.

At the center of this process are clear roles and responsibilities. Product leaders define outcomes and prioritize value; project managers coordinate planning, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communication; developers build and validate features; QA focuses on quality and acceptance criteria; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. This structure keeps delivery coordinated while creating a predictable rhythm for decision-making and status reporting.

Communication is built around regular cadence and transparency. The team uses weekly PM/Product check-ins, team standups, demos, stakeholder updates, and escalation paths for blockers or cross-team dependencies. A single source of truth—such as the project README, risk register, or status notes—helps ensure the right information reaches the right audience and reduces confusion. The documentation also emphasizes fast escalation for risks that could affect delivery, security, or business outcomes.

Quality and continuous improvement are treated as core practices rather than isolated checkpoints. Teams rely on CI for tests and linting, maintain unit and integration coverage, run smoke tests before release, and apply security scanning to reduce risk. Release work includes deployment checklists, rollback planning, and post-deploy verification. After each sprint or significant milestone, the team conducts a retrospective to capture lessons, assign ownership, and convert improvements into backlog items or action plans.
